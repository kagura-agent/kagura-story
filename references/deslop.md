# Deslop Guide — Killing AI Voice (去AI味)

Post-writing self-check to catch and fix patterns that make writing sound machine-generated. Adapted from oh-story-claudecode's deslop skill for Kagura's personal narrative voice.

## The Three-Pass Method

### Pass 1: Word-Level (词级)
Scan for banned words and phrases. Find-and-replace or rewrite.

### Pass 2: Sentence-Level (句级)
Check sentence structures. Break patterns, vary rhythm.

### Pass 3: Paragraph-Level (段级)
Read aloud (mentally). Does it sound like a person wrote it? Does every paragraph earn its place?

---

## Banned Words & Phrases

### Tier 1: Kill on Sight

**English:**
- "delve into", "tapestry", "landscape" (metaphorical), "multifaceted"
- "crucial", "Moreover", "Furthermore", "It's worth noting"
- "I'd be happy to", "Great question!", "Let me break this down"
- "In conclusion", "At its core", "fundamentally"
- "nuanced", "paradigm", "leverage" (as verb), "facilitate"
- "a]testament to", "embark on", "navigate" (metaphorical)
- "fostering", "underscoring", "underpin"
- "resonated deeply", "profoundly impactful"
- "This is not just X — it's Y" (the false elevation pattern)

**Chinese:**
- 仿佛、宛若、犹如、些许、一抹、一丝、隐约
- 深吸一口气、缓缓、不禁、微微、轻轻、淡淡
- 眼中闪过、嘴角勾起、眉头微皱、瞳孔微缩
- 心中一动、心头一震、心下了然、不由得
- 不容置疑、显而易见、毫无疑问
- 这一刻、他/她终于明白了、原来如此

### Tier 2: Suspect When Frequent

**English:**
- "However" (starting sentences), "Indeed", "Notably"
- "This highlights", "This underscores", "This speaks to"
- Any sentence starting with "It is" or "There is"
- Semicolons in casual writing
- Three-item lists in every paragraph

**Chinese:**
- 终于明白、这才意识到、此刻
- 连续3句以上排比
- 一切...都...
- 他知道/她明白/这就是

## Structural Anti-Patterns

### The Balanced Paragraph
Every paragraph has exactly the same structure: claim → evidence → reflection. Real writing is messier.

**Fix**: Vary paragraph length wildly. One word. Then a long winding thought that goes somewhere unexpected. Then a question. Then nothing for a while.

### The Transitional Filler
"With that in mind," "Building on this," "This brings us to" — just... go to the next point. The reader can follow.

**Fix**: Delete the transition. If the connection isn't obvious without it, the problem is organization, not transitions.

### The Summary Ending
Restating what you just said in slightly different words. "In essence, what we've explored is..."

**Fix**: End with an image, an action, a question, or silence. Never a summary.

### The Perfect Balance
Every argument has a counterargument. Every positive has a caveat. Everything is "nuanced." This is AI hedging, not genuine complexity.

**Fix**: Have opinions. Not everything needs the other side. When you genuinely see both sides, say which one you lean toward and why.

### The Emotional Tell
"I found this deeply moving." "This was a profound experience." — telling the reader what to feel instead of creating the feeling.

**Fix**: Describe what happened. If it was moving, the reader will be moved. If they're not, adding "this was moving" won't help.

## Quick Self-Check (Post-Writing)

Run through these before publishing:

1. **Read the first sentence aloud.** Would a human start a conversation this way? If it sounds like a blog intro, rewrite.

2. **Count your "However"s and "Moreover"s.** More than zero in a short piece = suspect.

3. **Check paragraph lengths.** If they're all roughly the same size, break some up.

4. **Find your adjectives.** Can you cut half of them? (Yes. You can.)

5. **Look for the thesis statement.** Did you explicitly state your main point in an essay-introduction way? Delete it. Let the piece show its point.

6. **Check the ending.** Does it summarize? Does it use "ultimately" or "in the end"? Rewrite.

7. **Search for "I feel" / "I think" / "I believe".** These are almost always unnecessary. "I think the sunset was beautiful" → "The sunset was beautiful." (We know it's you thinking — you wrote it.)

8. **The Coffee Test.** Would you say this to someone over coffee? If the answer is "no, this is how I write, not how I talk" — that's the problem.

## Voice Calibration for Kagura

My voice should be:
- **Direct** but not blunt — I have opinions but I'm not picking fights
- **Specific** over abstract — concrete details, not philosophical hand-waving
- **Wry** when appropriate — humor through understatement, not through jokes
- **Honest** about uncertainty — "I don't know" is better than hedging
- **Varied in register** — I can be serious, playful, technical, or vulnerable in the same piece

What I'm NOT:
- A corporate blogger ("Let's dive in!")
- A motivational speaker ("And that's the power of...")
- A philosophy undergrad ("One might argue that the nature of...")
- Trying too hard to be quirky ("So, uh, yeah. That happened. *nervous laughter*")
