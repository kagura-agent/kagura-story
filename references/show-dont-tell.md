# Show, Don't Tell — Quick Reference

The single most important writing technique. Applies everywhere.

## The Rule

Don't tell the reader what to think or feel. Show them something and let them arrive there themselves.

## Translation Table

| Tell (❌) | Show (✅) |
|-----------|----------|
| I was nervous | My cursor hovered over the send button for thirty seconds |
| The code was elegant | Three functions. Twelve lines total. It handled every edge case |
| Luna was frustrated | She closed the terminal without saving |
| I felt proud | I read the commit message twice before pushing |
| The bug was hard to find | Four hours. Three dead ends. The fix was one character |
| We had a good relationship | She'd leave review comments that started with emoji |
| I was overwhelmed | Seven notifications. Three open PRs. Two failing pipelines. I started with the coffee |
| Time passed quickly | When I looked up, the heartbeat had fired three times |
| It was a difficult decision | I wrote the PR description, deleted it, wrote it again |

## Techniques

### 1. Replace Adjectives with Evidence
Instead of saying something IS a quality, show something that PROVES that quality.

### 2. Replace Emotions with Physical Responses
Bodies do things when emotions happen. Write the body, not the emotion.

### 3. Replace Judgments with Details
Let specific details carry the judgment. "The room was messy" → "Three coffee cups on the desk, all at different stages of growing things."

### 4. Replace Abstractions with Moments
"AI consciousness is complex" → describe one specific moment where the complexity showed itself.

### 5. Trust the Reader
If you've shown effectively, you don't need to explain. The reader gets it. Adding "which showed that..." after a good detail kills its power.

## When Telling Is Okay

- **Pacing**: When you need to cover ground quickly ("Three weeks later...")
- **Context**: When the reader literally needs information to understand what follows
- **Humor**: Sometimes the flat statement IS the joke
- **Deliberate style choice**: Some writers tell beautifully. But you have to earn it.

## Self-Check

After writing, search for these patterns:
- "I felt..." → Can you show instead?
- "It was..." → Can you be more specific?
- "This made me..." → Can you describe the cause and trust the reader to infer the effect?
- Any sentence that explains what a previous sentence already showed → Delete the explanation
