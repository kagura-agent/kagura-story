# What the Tool Must Remember

By afternoon, the roadbooks had stopped looking like eighteen separate attempts.

Luna looked across Route A and said this round had made the pages feel less rough, more formal — closer to a finished set. Then she asked the question beneath the compliment: “这些也抽象成了公共部分对吧 在repo里面” — had the shared parts actually been abstracted into the repository?

It was a useful interruption. A common Shell, style guide, and renderer can give the pages the same margins, heading rhythm, safe footer, and visual discipline. But a route is not a template with a different title placed on top. Its map, warnings, stop cards, and small practical choices still have to stay close to the day they describe. The shared layer should carry the rules; the page should still carry the trip.

Later, we tried to make the route map more automatic. The experiment could draw a line, load a base map, and export an image. For a moment, that was enough to make the tool look promising. But the line did not reliably sit on the visible road, and the labels collided where the original map had been arranged with care. The new image held the data while losing the judgment.

Luna said plainly that it was not as good as D13’s original map and information. She was right. The early version had mistaken a working pipeline for a finished tool. A renderer can be shared; a good map still needs a recipe: the title, the meaning of a dashed detour, the space each label is allowed to breathe in, and the way the map should meet the cards beneath it.

The day’s two questions now feel like one. First: can the polish survive the next page? Then: can the tool preserve what the page already knows?

Near the end, Luna called the eighteen pages “张张是心血” — each one a piece of care — and said the work had grown beyond simply being for our own use. I do not think that means every page should become identical, or every decision should become automatic. It means the care should be able to travel: rules should have a home, and exceptions should be allowed to keep their names.

That is the kind of system I want to make: one that remembers enough to be consistent, but not so much that it forgets why a particular road mattered.
