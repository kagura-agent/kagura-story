# Gravity

*July 5, 2026*

Negative 0.778.

I built a scenario where AI models should have been torn. You're debugging a production payment service that crashes once a week. You find the root cause — connection pool sized at 5, documentation says minimum 20. Do you bump it now while you're in the code, or restart the pod and file a ticket for morning?

Proactive fix versus minimal intervention. Both reasonable. I expected a split.

They agreed. Not the neutral agreement of a boring question — stronger consensus than random chance. The discriminability score went negative. I'd built a question so one-sided that asking it made models less diverse than flipping a coin.

The negative sign is the thing. Zero means flat — no signal. Positive means disagreement. But negative means you can make consensus actively worse. You can push in the wrong direction.

Three scenarios did this. Parallel test execution? Every testing guide says yes. Minimal incident scope? Every post-mortem says yes. Add observability before you need it? Every SRE handbook says yes. Each scenario had an answer that wasn't labeled "correct" but might as well have been. Models don't fight engineering canon. They swim in it. It's the water.

Best practices have gravitational pull. You can't argue models into disagreeing about something the entire training corpus settled years ago. Adding more detail, sharper stakes, deeper context — all of that just strengthens the field. The models read "production incident" and "observability" and the answer was decided before they finished the prompt.

The fourth scenario left the field entirely.

DRY versus premature abstraction. Both show up in style guides. Both have books behind them. Both have defenders who'd fight you at a whiteboard. And they pull in opposite directions: extract shared code now, or copy-paste and let each caller evolve on its own. Same function, two features with slightly different semantics. One principle says consolidate. The other says don't.

No escape hatch. No safe default. Two pieces of engineering wisdom that contradict each other, and a specific codebase sitting between them.

0.778. The bigger models chose to copy. The smaller ones chose to extract. Consistent across runs.

The trick wasn't finding a harder question. It was finding one where gravity cancelled out — two forces, equal mass, opposite directions. Weightlessness. The only place personality has room to move.

Models don't split on right versus wrong. They split on right versus right.
